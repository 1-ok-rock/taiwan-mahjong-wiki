Basic Rules: 16 Tiles and 17 Tiles

Taiwanese mahjong usually has 16 structural tiles between turns and 17 in a standard winning hand.

Key idea: Five melds and one pair make the standard 17-tile winning structure.

Sequences, triplets, and pairs

A sequence has three consecutive numbered tiles of the same suit. A triplet has three identical tiles. A pair consists of two identical tiles. A Kong has four identical tiles but counts as one meld.

Example: Five melds and one pair
1 Character2 Character3 Character
4 Character5 Character6 Character
2 Dot3 Dot4 Dot
7 Bamboo8 Bamboo9 Bamboo
EastEastEast
White DragonWhite Dragon

3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 2 = 17 structural tiles.

Taking turns

The dealer commonly starts with 17 structural tiles and discards first. Other players draw and discard in turn, subject to permitted calls.

Kongs and flower tiles

Kongs contain four physical tiles yet count as one meld, normally requiring a replacement draw. Flower tiles are bonus tiles usually revealed and replaced rather than counted as structural tiles.

House rules: Scoring, minimum winning requirements and special hands can differ. Check the rules at your table.
